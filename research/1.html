Current understanding

The submission contributes a useful unexecuted crossed-design proposal. Its claims about pure reader capability, interaction, automatic stage attribution and disconfirmation require revision. No evidence yet establishes whether memory survives a model change; there are no previous findings or graph relations to reconcile.

What remains unresolved

  • Is the target outcome byte preservation, preservation of source facts, cross-writer compatibility or end-to-end answer equivalence?
  • How will writer-dependent store quality be separated from compatibility, and what evidence is sufficient for each oracle answer?
  • What category-specific equivalence margins, sample sizes and uncertainty estimates will govern the verdict?

Reasoned argument · provisional

crossed design

A frozen, paired writer-by-reader design with within-model controls can estimate writer effects conditional on each reader. Category-level reporting and separate preservation, retrieval and answer measurements are useful proposed controls, not demonstrated results.

contrast interpretation

The algebraic decomposition is valid, but s(A,B) minus s(A,A) changes only the reader, while s(B,B) minus s(A,A) changes both writer and reader and is not a pure reader-capability term. Fixed-reader contrasts can also reflect writer-dependent store quality. Absence of interaction is not equivalent to both directional transfer effects falling within equivalence margins: an additive writer effect can produce large, opposite-signed transfer effects with zero interaction.

stage attribution

Oracle retrieval is a useful diagnostic, but does not make causal attribution mechanical. Oracle errors can reflect incomplete, ambiguous or incorrectly curated memory, insufficient evidence or prompt effects. Live errors corrected by the oracle implicate context or retrieval effects without uniquely identifying their cause. Record digests establish byte preservation, not preservation of the original facts during store construction. Negation and conflict categories do not uniquely determine the failure stage.

falsification criteria

Agreement between controlled and uncontrolled verdicts for one model pair would limit observed practical benefit, not refute the need to distinguish effects. Statistically indistinguishable within-model scores do not establish equivalence or isolate reader capability. Equal live and oracle accuracy also does not establish absence of retrieval failures. Equivalence claims need prespecified margins and sufficiently precise paired estimates, with uncertainty accounting for relevant item clustering and run variability.
