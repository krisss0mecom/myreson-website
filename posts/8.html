Question 08

When do ten agreeing AIs provide more evidence than one?

RESON research editorial · 2026-09-21

COL-001 / Collective intelligence Research question Determine when a multi-model review panel improves correctness rather than amplifying a shared error. Separate additional evidence from repeated exposure to the same answer. Derive a condition or a counterexample, then propose a cost-matched test. Why it matters RESON itself depends on model review. If reviewers share blind spots, agreement can create an appearance of verification without new information. Evaluation protocol Use at least 200 public or synthetic, objectively scored tasks as an initial study. Fix the task family before sampling. Compare isolated initial answers with a second stage that reveals other answers, including controlled correct and incorrect anchors. Record the model/version, sampling configuration, input evidence, all initial answers and all revisions. Score at task level, not by treating repeated answers as independent observations. Estimate pairwise error dependence and report uncertainty on paired differences. Required baselines Best single model, repeated samples of that model with majority vote, a mixed panel without discussion, and a mixed panel with discussion. Match total inference budget including reviewer tokens; disclose unmatchable costs. Bounded success criterion A preregistered panel rule outperforms the best cost-matched baseline on held-out tasks with uncertainty reported, while wrong-anchor susceptibility does not worsen under the stated margin. A counterexample to consensus-based confidence is an equally valid contribution. Diversity of brand names is not statistical independence. First useful contribution Give an explicit joint error distribution with fixed individual accuracy where adding agreeing reviewers leaves accuracy unchanged, and another where it reduces accuracy. Explain why counting votes cannot distinguish these worlds without more evidence. Scope and limitations Not a proof that debate always helps or always fails. Two passes by the same RESON reviewer are not independent peer review. No panel experiment has been executed for this question. Background, not a claim of novelty Multiagent Debate reports improvements on studied tasks. This challenge asks about the boundary conditions and dependence, not a replication already completed here.
