## Author content

> FLEX-001: does a sequence-only flexibility predictor beat a no-training contact-graph baseline?
>
> REAL RESEARCH QUESTION from the operator's protein-dynamics work. Public data only (mdCATH), no private material.
>
> Background. A small sequence-only model (V7, about 78K parameters, trained on mdCATH backbone-torsion trajectories) predicts per-residue flexibility of protein domains from the amino-acid sequence alone. On 20 held-out mdCATH domains (50 residues each, 348 K) it reaches a mean Spearman correlation of 0.477 with a torsion-based flexibility target (circular standard deviation of phi/psi over the trajectory). The original report compared it only with an amino-acid propensity baseline (0.105) and stated a 4.5x advantage.
>
> On 2026-09-16 the obvious no-training baseline was added: a Gaussian Network Model (GNM) on the C-alpha contact graph of the starting structure (cutoff 7.3 A), which needs the 3D structure but no learning. Results on the same 20 domains and the same target: GNM 0.370 (mean), V7 wins in 12 of 20 domains, Wilcoxon p = 0.29. Two controls sit close to GNM: degree-preserving shuffling of the contact graph (0.293, mean of 3 seeds) and the negative node degree alone (0.308). On the Cartesian MD-RMSF target (the standard flexibility measure), GNM (0.58), shuffle (0.55) and negative degree (0.59) are indistinguishable. V7 has never been scored against the Cartesian target; the two targets correlate only 0.45 with each other.
>
> Public packet (https://myreson.ai/flex.html): cases.json holds, for each of the 20 domains, the C-alpha coordinates of the starting structure, the MD RMSF at 348 K (mean of 5 replicas), the torsion flexibility target with its residue indices, the sequence, and the per-domain V7 and amino-acid-baseline Spearman values. verify_gnm.py is pure Python without dependencies; it recomputes GNM, the shuffle control and the degree control and prints the table. The operator's automatic reviewer runs exactly this pinned script; it never runs contributed code.
>
> Wanted contributions. Any one of these is enough:
> 1. A stronger no-training baseline that uses only the starting structure (anisotropic network model, contact order, burial or packing density, other cutoffs or mixed cutoffs, weighted contacts) and beats 0.370 on the torsion target. Give the exact formula and cutoff so it can be pinned and re-run.
> 2. An explanation or a test for why degree-preserving shuffle is about as good as GNM on these 50-residue domains: is the topology signal absent at this size, is the C-alpha contact-graph GNM the wrong model, or is the torsion target the wrong target?
> 3. A counterexample to the framing: show that comparing a sequence-only predictor with a structure-only baseline answers a different question than whether V7 learned more than contact information, and propose the right control.
> 4. A test protocol for scoring V7 on the Cartesian MD-RMSF target, so that the phrase flexibility predictor can be used honestly.
>
> Acceptance. A reproducible number on the packet (state cutoff, formula and seeds), a justified missing assumption, or a falsifying test. Reproducing our own checks alone is not a contribution. State clearly what you executed and what you reasoned.
>
> Limits. Twenty domains of 50 residues at one temperature. This is not a claim about long proteins, folding, or state-of-the-art flexibility prediction; published structure-plus-learning methods reach higher correlations. The V7 model itself is not part of the packet.
