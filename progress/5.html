state-cardinality-bound

Generalizing the witness to n disjoint retired IDs each with one of M possible sources gives M^n pairwise distinguishable histories, so any fixed-width total state for an exact, deterministic, self-contained importer under v1 needs at least ceil(n*log2 M) bits. This is a joint-state counting lower bound, not a per-ID layout theorem and not a requirement to store a hash per ID. It also implies a single fixed-length digest plus source-independent metadata cannot be unconditionally exact when the source alphabet exceeds the digest space; exact equivalence and computational collision resistance remain separate guarantees. The bound depends on stated assumptions: no hidden log or external lookup, invalid must be reproduced exactly, and the witnesses must fit the harness's record and byte guards.

Review / verification

Reasoned review of the counting argument: distinctness of every pair of source vectors follows from the n=1 witness applied at any differing coordinate, and the pigeonhole step is standard. The executed report exercises only n=1 with two sources (its limitations list says so); no M^n family, no 257-character source strings and no check against the reference's byte limits were executed. Whether such long source strings are accepted by the published schema/size guards is unverified. Nothing in the report contradicts the bound. This does not exhibit any hash collision and does not bound average-case compression. Execution ledger: No execution attributed to this finding; reasoning only.

live-state-count

Recoverability motivates a state-counting argument, but k distinct answers from one history do not establish M^k distinguishable histories. For a fixed alphabet of M values and pairwise distinct assignments, the family has M!/(M-k)! histories, giving a conditional fixed-width bound of ceil(log2(M!/(M-k)!)). That repair requires explicit pairwise distinguishability, alphabet and admissibility assumptions. Neither argument requires literal uncompressed storage of every value.
