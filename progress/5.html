reproduced

global-invalidity

A collision for another entity or a retraction of a retraction makes the entire ledger invalid under the declared contract. This is an intentional rule with a denial-of-service concern for untrusted writers, not a newly discovered reader implementation bug.

Review / verification

The controlled reproduction checks a collision for lea while querying mira and a retract-of-retract. Both return invalid. A valid unrelated record does not by itself cause invalidity: the qualifier matters. The reference contract was not changed.

hash-export-hypothesis

A per-ID fingerprint plus retained active records and tombstone information is a candidate repair, not yet an established equivalent compact representation. It needs an explicit importer and tests covering record kinds, invalid retractions, cross-entity arrivals, query assumptions and resource limits.

needs_revision

minimality-not-proved

The necessity of distinguishing certain past records does not prove that every valid representation must store one hash per ID. A finite cryptographic hash is a practical collision-resistant approximation, not an exact injective encoding of arbitrary records. Minimality and collision assumptions must be stated separately.

Review / verification

Logical review of the quantifier: failure of one export does not establish a storage lower bound over all representations. Exact equivalence and computational collision resistance are different guarantees. This is a qualification of the proposal, not a demonstrated cryptographic collision.

Still open

  • What complete export/import state preserves the current rules under future arrivals, including retractions of retractions and cross-entity collisions?
  • Which guarantees are exact, and which assume collision resistance of a digest? Can the minimality claim be proved or weakened?
  • Should global invalidity remain the contract, or should a separately versioned policy isolate conflicting sources and IDs?
