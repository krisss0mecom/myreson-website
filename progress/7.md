# Problem 7: Can an AI know when not to answer after the world changes?

## Original problem

CAL-001 / Reliability

Research question
Find a selective-answering rule that keeps the error rate among answered questions below 5% after a specified distribution shift, while answering at least 50% of questions. State which shifts and assumptions make this possible, or prove a limitation. These are proposed challenge targets, not measured RESON results.

Why it matters
A system that confidently answers the wrong question is not made reliable by a more fluent explanation. Uncertainty estimates need testing outside the calibration population.

Evaluation protocol
Use public or synthetic question-answer pairs with a checkable answer key. Freeze a calibration split and a disjoint evaluation split before choosing a threshold. Evaluate separately under label-preserving paraphrase, changed topic mix and unavailable evidence. Define the sampling unit. Start with at least 200 evaluation items per declared regime, disclose all exclusions and abstentions, and report the answered-item count, conditional error, coverage and a one-sided 95% error bound. This starting size is not a claim of adequate power for every subgroup. An aggregate score must not hide a failing subgroup.

Required baselines
Compare always-answer, a fixed confidence threshold and repeated-answer disagreement at matched inference cost. If probabilities are unavailable, label the proxy and do not call it calibrated probability.

Bounded success criterion
For the declared target population, the one-sided 95% upper error bound is at most 5% at coverage of at least 50%. Publish a transfer failure as a useful negative result. No finite test establishes reliability under arbitrary future shift.

First useful contribution
Construct two populations with the same observable confidence scores but different answer correctness. State exactly what additional assumption would make a transfer guarantee possible.

Scope and limitations
Not a cure for hallucination, a guarantee under arbitrary shift, or a medical decision system. No model evaluation has been run for this new challenge.

Background, not a claim of novelty
Semantic Uncertainty studies meaning-aware uncertainty. It motivates the baseline, not the new target or a distribution-shift guarantee.
